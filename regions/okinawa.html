Okinawa

Japan's tropical paradise — turquoise seas, Ryukyu heritage, and island time

Top Sightseeing Spots

Naha

Shuri Castle

The reconstructed palace of the Ryukyu Kingdom, Shuri Castle blends Chinese and Japanese architectural styles with its distinctive red walls and ornate throne room. A UNESCO World Heritage Site, it served as the political and cultural center of the Ryukyu Kingdom for over 400 years.

Zamami / Tokashiki

Kerama Islands

This national park archipelago about 30 kilometers west of Naha boasts some of Japan's clearest waters, nicknamed "Kerama Blue." The islands offer world-class snorkeling and diving with sea turtles, coral reefs, and humpback whale watching from January to March.

Motobu, Okinawa

Churaumi Aquarium

One of the world's largest and most impressive aquariums, Churaumi features the massive Kuroshio Sea tank housing whale sharks and manta rays. Located on the Motobu Peninsula in northern Okinawa, it offers breathtaking views of the ocean and nearby Ie Island.

Naha

Kokusai Street

Naha's main thoroughfare stretches 1.6 kilometers and is packed with shops, restaurants, and entertainment. Known as the "Miracle Mile" for its rapid post-war recovery, the street is the best place to sample Okinawan cuisine like goya champuru, soki soba, and purple sweet potato tarts.

Ishigaki Island

Kabira Bay

Widely considered one of the most beautiful bays in Japan, Kabira Bay features stunning emerald-green waters dotted with small islands covered in lush vegetation. Swimming is not permitted due to strong currents, but glass-bottom boat tours reveal the vibrant coral and tropical fish below.

Nanjo, Okinawa

Sefa Utaki

The most sacred spiritual site in Okinawan religion, Sefa Utaki is a UNESCO World Heritage Site where Ryukyuan priestesses once performed rituals. The natural rock formations create a triangular opening that frames a view of Kudaka Island, believed to be the island where the gods descended.

Getting There

Naha Airport on Okinawa's main island receives frequent flights from Tokyo (2.5 hours), Osaka (2 hours), and other major Japanese cities. The Kerama Islands are accessible by ferry or high-speed boat from Naha's Tomari Port in 30 to 90 minutes. Ishigaki Island, gateway to the Yaeyama Islands and Kabira Bay, is a 1-hour flight from Naha. There is no train or shinkansen connection to Okinawa.

Best Time to Visit

The beach season runs from April to October, with the best weather from May to June (before the rainy season ends) and September to October. Humpback whales visit the Kerama Islands from January to March. The rainy season (tsuyu) typically lasts from mid-May to mid-June. Typhoon season peaks from August to October. Winter temperatures rarely drop below 15 degrees Celsius, making Okinawa pleasant year-round.
