Kansai

The cultural soul of Japan — ancient capitals, sacred shrines, and legendary street food

Top Sightseeing Spots

Kyoto

Fushimi Inari Shrine

Famous for its thousands of vermillion torii gates winding up Mount Inari, this Shinto shrine is dedicated to the god of rice and commerce. The full hike through the gates to the summit takes about two hours and offers serene forest paths and city views.

Kyoto

Kinkaku-ji

The Golden Pavilion is one of Kyoto's most iconic landmarks, its top two floors covered entirely in gold leaf and perfectly reflected in the surrounding mirror pond. Originally built in 1397 as a retirement villa for Shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu, it became a Zen temple after his death.

Osaka

Osaka Castle

This towering castle, originally built by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1583, is a symbol of Osaka's resilience. The reconstructed main tower houses a museum on the castle's history, and the surrounding park is one of the city's best cherry blossom spots.

Nara

Nara Park

Home to over 1,000 free-roaming sacred deer, Nara Park encompasses some of Japan's most significant temples, including Todai-ji with its enormous bronze Great Buddha. The deer, considered divine messengers, bow politely to visitors offering them special crackers.

Hyogo Prefecture

Himeji Castle

Widely regarded as Japan's most spectacular castle, this UNESCO World Heritage Site is nicknamed "White Heron Castle" for its elegant white exterior. It is one of the few original castles remaining in Japan, having survived wars, earthquakes, and air raids for over 400 years.

Wakayama Prefecture

Koyasan

The headquarters of Shingon Buddhism, Mount Koya is a sacred temple town set atop a secluded mountain. Visitors can stay overnight in temple lodgings (shukubo), walk through the atmospheric Okunoin cemetery, and experience morning prayer ceremonies with the monks.

Getting There

Kansai International Airport serves the region with international and domestic flights. The Tokaido Shinkansen connects Tokyo to Kyoto in 2 hours 15 minutes and to Osaka in 2 hours 30 minutes. Nara is a 45-minute train ride from both Kyoto and Osaka. Himeji is about 1 hour west of Osaka by shinkansen, and Koyasan can be reached by train and cable car from Osaka in about 2 hours.

Best Time to Visit

Spring (late March to mid-April) is peak cherry blossom season, transforming temple grounds and castle parks into canopies of pink. Autumn (mid-November to early December) brings vibrant maple foliage, especially stunning at Kyoto's Tofuku-ji and Eikando temples. Summer hosts lively festivals including Kyoto's Gion Matsuri in July and Osaka's Tenjin Matsuri. Winter offers peaceful temple visits with fewer crowds.
