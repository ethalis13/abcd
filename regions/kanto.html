Kanto

The beating heart of modern Japan — where neon-lit megacity meets timeless tradition

Top Sightseeing Spots

Tokyo

Tokyo Tower & Skytree

Tokyo Tower, the iconic 1958 red-and-white landmark, and Tokyo Skytree, the world's tallest broadcasting tower at 634 meters, both offer sweeping panoramic views of the sprawling metropolis and beyond to Mount Fuji on clear days.

Tochigi Prefecture

Nikko Toshogu

This lavishly decorated UNESCO World Heritage shrine complex is the mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu, the founder of the Tokugawa shogunate. Its ornate carvings include the famous "see no evil, speak no evil, hear no evil" monkeys.

Kanagawa Prefecture

Kamakura Great Buddha

The 13-meter-tall bronze Great Buddha at Kotoku-in temple has sat in the open air since a tsunami washed away its hall in 1498. Kamakura, the former medieval capital, is also home to dozens of temples and shrines nestled among forested hills.

Kanagawa Prefecture

Hakone

A premier hot spring resort town nestled in the mountains southwest of Tokyo, Hakone offers open-air baths, the Hakone Open-Air Museum, scenic boat cruises on Lake Ashi, and stunning views of Mount Fuji on clear days.

Kanagawa Prefecture

Yokohama Chinatown

The largest Chinatown in Japan and one of the largest in the world, Yokohama Chinatown is home to over 500 shops and restaurants. The vibrant streets are filled with Chinese cuisine, colorful gates, and the ornate Kanteibyo Temple.

Tokyo

Senso-ji Temple

Tokyo's oldest and most visited temple, Senso-ji in Asakusa dates back to 645 AD. The approach through the iconic Kaminarimon (Thunder Gate) and the bustling Nakamise shopping street is one of the most photographed scenes in Japan.

Getting There

Tokyo is served by two major airports: Narita International Airport (60-90 minutes from central Tokyo) and Haneda Airport (20-30 minutes from the city center). The Tokaido Shinkansen connects Tokyo to Osaka in 2.5 hours. Within the Kanto region, an extensive rail network including JR lines and private railways makes getting around easy and efficient.

Best Time to Visit

Spring (late March to mid-April) brings stunning cherry blossoms to parks and temple grounds throughout the region. Autumn (mid-November to early December) offers brilliant foliage, especially in Nikko and Kamakura. Summer is hot and humid but alive with festivals and fireworks displays. Winter is mild and clear, offering the best chances for views of Mount Fuji from Tokyo and Hakone.
