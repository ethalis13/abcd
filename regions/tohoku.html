Tohoku

Japan's hidden heartland — ancient temples, hot spring villages, and unspoiled mountain scenery

Top Sightseeing Spots

Miyagi Prefecture

Matsushima Bay

Regarded as one of Japan's three most scenic views, Matsushima Bay is dotted with over 260 pine-covered islands. Visitors can cruise among the islands and explore centuries-old Zuigan-ji Temple nearby.

Aomori Prefecture

Hirosaki Castle

This Edo-period castle is surrounded by one of Japan's finest cherry blossom parks, with over 2,600 cherry trees. During spring the moat becomes blanketed with pink petals, creating an unforgettable scene.

Yamagata Prefecture

Ginzan Onsen

This fairy-tale hot spring village features wooden ryokan inns lining a narrow river gorge. Gas-lit streets and snow-covered rooftops in winter create a scene straight out of a Studio Ghibli film, said to have inspired "Spirited Away."

Yamagata Prefecture

Zao Onsen

Famous for its "snow monsters" — trees encased in thick snow and ice forming eerie sculptural shapes on the mountainside. Zao is also a popular ski resort and hot spring destination with a striking emerald-green crater lake.

Miyagi Prefecture

Sendai

Known as the "City of Trees," Sendai is Tohoku's largest city and gateway to the region. It is famous for its grilled beef tongue cuisine and the dazzling Tanabata Festival held every August with elaborate paper streamers.

Yamagata Prefecture

Yamadera Temple

Officially named Risshaku-ji, this mountain temple requires climbing over 1,000 stone steps through ancient cedar forest. The summit rewards visitors with breathtaking panoramic views of the valley below, inspiring the famous haiku poet Matsuo Basho.

Getting There

The Tohoku Shinkansen connects Tokyo to Sendai in about 1.5 hours and continues north to Morioka and Shin-Aomori. Local trains and buses serve smaller destinations, though a rental car is useful for reaching remote hot spring towns like Ginzan Onsen. Sendai Airport also offers domestic flights from major cities.

Best Time to Visit

Summer (June to August) is festival season, with Sendai Tanabata, Aomori Nebuta, and Akita Kanto among Japan's most spectacular celebrations. Winter (December to March) brings deep snow ideal for skiing and the magical snow monster scenery at Zao. Cherry blossoms arrive in late April, and autumn colors peak from late October to early November.
