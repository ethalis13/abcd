Hokkaido

Japan's wild northern frontier — untamed nature, fresh seafood, and powder snow

Top Sightseeing Spots

Sapporo

Sapporo

Hokkaido's vibrant capital is famous for its annual Snow Festival, beer gardens, and outstanding ramen. The city blends urban sophistication with easy access to nearby ski resorts and natural hot springs.

Otaru

Otaru Canal

This picturesque canal district features beautifully preserved stone warehouses dating from the early 1900s. At night the gas lamps along the canal create an enchanting atmosphere, especially during the winter Snow Light Path Festival.

Furano

Furano Lavender Fields

From late June through August, the rolling hills of Furano transform into a sea of purple lavender. Farm Tomita is the most iconic spot, offering stunning floral landscapes and lavender-flavored soft serve ice cream.

Shari District

Shiretoko National Park

A UNESCO World Heritage Site at the northeastern tip of Hokkaido, Shiretoko is one of Japan's most pristine wilderness areas. Home to brown bears, deer, and sea eagles, it offers dramatic cliffs, waterfalls, and drift ice in winter.

Hakodate

Hakodate

Perched at Hokkaido's southern tip, Hakodate boasts one of the world's top three night views from Mount Hakodate. Its morning fish market serves some of the freshest seafood in Japan, and the historic Motomachi district features charming Western-style buildings.

Noboribetsu

Noboribetsu Onsen

One of Hokkaido's premier hot spring resorts, Noboribetsu features Jigokudani (Hell Valley), a volcanic crater with steaming vents and sulfurous streams. The town offers a variety of mineral-rich baths with different therapeutic properties.

Getting There

Fly directly to New Chitose Airport (near Sapporo) from Tokyo in about 1.5 hours. The Hokkaido Shinkansen connects Tokyo to Hakodate in roughly 4 hours. From Hakodate, limited express trains reach Sapporo in about 3.5 hours. Rental cars are highly recommended for exploring Hokkaido's vast countryside and national parks.

Best Time to Visit

Winter (December to February) is ideal for skiing, snowboarding, and the Sapporo Snow Festival in early February. Summer (June to August) brings mild temperatures perfect for hiking, cycling, and the Furano lavender season. Autumn (September to October) offers brilliant foliage, while spring brings cherry blossoms to Hokkaido later than the rest of Japan, typically in early May.
