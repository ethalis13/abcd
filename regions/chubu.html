Chubu

The roof of Japan — majestic alps, historic villages, and the iconic Mount Fuji

Top Sightseeing Spots

Shizuoka / Yamanashi Prefecture

Mount Fuji

Japan's tallest peak at 3,776 meters is an iconic UNESCO World Heritage Site and a sacred symbol of the nation. The official climbing season runs from July to September, while the Fuji Five Lakes area offers year-round views and outdoor activities.

Gifu Prefecture

Shirakawa-go

This UNESCO World Heritage village is renowned for its steep thatched-roof farmhouses (gassho-zukuri) designed to withstand heavy snowfall. In winter, the village is illuminated at night, creating one of Japan's most magical scenes.

Ishikawa Prefecture

Kanazawa Kenroku-en

Ranked among Japan's three most beautiful landscape gardens, Kenroku-en features ponds, streams, waterfalls, and teahouses across its spacious grounds. Each season brings different beauty, from snow-laden pine trees in winter to cherry blossoms and irises in spring.

Gifu Prefecture

Takayama Old Town

The beautifully preserved Sanmachi Suji district features Edo-period merchant houses, sake breweries, and traditional craft shops. Takayama's morning markets and local Hida beef are highlights, along with its spectacular spring and autumn festivals.

Nagano Prefecture

Matsumoto Castle

One of Japan's finest original castles, Matsumoto Castle is known as the "Crow Castle" for its striking black exterior. Built in the late 16th century, it is one of only five castles designated as a National Treasure, with a stunning alpine backdrop.

Aichi Prefecture

Nagoya Castle

Originally built by Tokugawa Ieyasu in 1612, Nagoya Castle is famous for its golden shachihoko (dolphin-fish) roof ornaments. The reconstructed Honmaru Palace showcases exquisite painted sliding doors and screens reflecting the grandeur of the Edo period.

Getting There

The Hokuriku Shinkansen connects Tokyo to Kanazawa in about 2.5 hours via Nagano. Nagoya is on the Tokaido Shinkansen line, roughly 1 hour 40 minutes from Tokyo. Takayama and Shirakawa-go are accessible by limited express trains and buses from Nagoya or Kanazawa. Mount Fuji can be reached by bus from Tokyo's Shinjuku Station in about 2 hours.

Best Time to Visit

Autumn (October to November) paints the Japanese Alps in spectacular red and gold foliage. Winter (December to March) transforms Shirakawa-go into a snowy wonderland and offers world-class skiing in Nagano and Niigata. The Mount Fuji climbing season runs July to September. Spring brings cherry blossoms to Takayama and Kanazawa in mid-April.
