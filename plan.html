Plan Your Trip to Japan

Everything you need to know to make the most of your Japanese adventure.

Getting Around

Japan Rail Pass (JR Pass)

The Japan Rail Pass is a cost-effective ticket for overseas visitors, offering unlimited travel on most JR trains nationwide, including the shinkansen. Available in 7-day, 14-day, and 21-day options, the JR Pass is ideal for travelers planning to cover long distances between cities like Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, and Hiroshima. Purchase and activate your pass before arriving or at major JR stations. A 7-day pass typically pays for itself after just one round trip between Tokyo and Kyoto.

Shinkansen (Bullet Train)

Japan's world-famous bullet trains travel at speeds up to 320 km/h, connecting major cities with remarkable punctuality. The Tokaido Shinkansen links Tokyo to Osaka via Nagoya and Kyoto in just over two hours. The Tohoku Shinkansen runs north from Tokyo to Sendai and Shin-Aomori, while the Hokuriku Shinkansen reaches Kanazawa. The Sanyo line extends west to Hiroshima and Hakata (Fukuoka). Reserved seats are recommended during peak travel seasons such as Golden Week, Obon, and New Year.

IC Cards (Suica / Pasmo)

Rechargeable IC cards like Suica and Pasmo are tap-and-go smart cards that work on virtually all local trains, subways, and buses across Japan. Simply load credit at any station ticket machine and tap the card at the gate. Beyond transit, IC cards are accepted at convenience stores, vending machines, coin lockers, and many shops. Pick one up at any major JR station or airport upon arrival — they are interchangeable nationwide regardless of brand.

Domestic Flights

For reaching distant destinations quickly, domestic flights are a practical choice. Airlines such as ANA, JAL, Peach, and Jetstar Japan offer frequent service between major cities. Flights are especially useful for getting to Hokkaido (Sapporo's New Chitose Airport), Okinawa (Naha Airport), and Kyushu (Fukuoka Airport) from Tokyo or Osaka. Budget carriers often have fares competitive with shinkansen tickets when booked in advance, and flight times are typically one to two hours.

Best Seasons to Visit

Spring (March – May)

Spring is one of the most popular times to visit Japan, thanks to the spectacular cherry blossom (sakura) season that sweeps across the country from late March through mid-April. Temperatures are mild and pleasant, perfect for walking and sightseeing. Be aware that Golden Week (late April to early May) is a major holiday period when domestic travel surges — book accommodation and transport well in advance. Top spring spots include Ueno Park in Tokyo, the Philosopher's Path in Kyoto, and Hirosaki Castle in Aomori.

Summer (June – August)

Summer brings hot and humid weather to most of Japan, but it is also the season of vibrant matsuri (festivals) and spectacular hanabi (fireworks) displays. The rainy season (tsuyu) typically runs from mid-June to mid-July in most regions. Head to Hokkaido for cooler temperatures, or enjoy the beaches of Okinawa and the Izu Peninsula. Major events include the Gion Matsuri in Kyoto, Nebuta Festival in Aomori, and the Sumida River Fireworks in Tokyo. Pack light, breathable clothing and stay hydrated.

Autumn (September – November)

Autumn offers some of the best travel conditions in Japan, with comfortable temperatures, clear skies, and fewer crowds than spring. The fall foliage (koyo) rivals the cherry blossoms in beauty, painting temple gardens and mountain landscapes in brilliant reds, oranges, and golds from mid-October through late November. Outstanding autumn destinations include Nikko, Kyoto's Tofuku-ji Temple, the Oirase Gorge in Tohoku, and the Japanese Alps around Kamikochi. Harvest season also brings excellent regional cuisine.

Winter (December – February)

Winter in Japan is a wonderland for skiers, onsen (hot spring) lovers, and fans of dazzling illumination displays. Hokkaido and the Japanese Alps receive abundant snowfall, offering world-class powder skiing at resorts like Niseko, Furano, and Hakuba. The Sapporo Snow Festival in February draws millions of visitors with its massive ice sculptures. Across the country, cities sparkle with winter illuminations. Warm up in an outdoor onsen surrounded by snow — an iconic Japanese experience. Southern regions like Kyushu and Okinawa remain mild.

Essential Tips

Money

Despite Japan's high-tech reputation, cash remains widely used, especially at smaller restaurants, temples, and local shops. Carry a reasonable amount of yen at all times. International credit and debit cards are accepted at major hotels, department stores, and chain restaurants. For cash withdrawals, 7-Eleven ATMs (Seven Bank) and Japan Post ATMs reliably accept foreign cards with no language barrier — they are available around the clock in most locations. The currency is the Japanese yen (JPY), and coins come in 1, 5, 10, 50, 100, and 500 yen denominations.

Language

While Japanese is the primary language, you can navigate tourist areas comfortably with English. Train stations, airports, and major attractions display signs in English (and often Chinese and Korean). Learning a few basic phrases goes a long way: "sumimasen" (excuse me), "arigatou gozaimasu" (thank you), and "eigo wa dekimasu ka?" (do you speak English?). Download Google Translate with the Japanese offline language pack before your trip — the camera translation feature is invaluable for reading menus and signs in real time.

Etiquette

Japanese culture places high value on respect and consideration for others. Always remove your shoes when entering homes, traditional ryokan inns, and some restaurants — look for a shoe rack or raised floor at the entrance. Tipping is not customary and can even cause confusion. On public transit, keep conversations quiet and set your phone to silent mode. Bowing is the standard greeting; a slight nod is sufficient for casual interactions. When using chopsticks, never stick them upright in rice or pass food directly between chopsticks, as these gestures are associated with funeral rites.

Connectivity

Staying connected in Japan is easy with several options available at the airport upon arrival. Pocket WiFi devices can be rented and picked up at Narita, Haneda, Kansai, or other major airports — they provide reliable 4G/5G connectivity for multiple devices and are returned by mail or at the airport before departure. Alternatively, prepaid travel SIM cards are sold at airport counters and electronics shops, offering data-only plans for 7 to 30 days. Free WiFi is available at most train stations, convenience stores, and shopping centers, though speeds and reliability vary.

Sample Itineraries

7 Days — Classic Japan

Tokyo → Hakone → Kyoto → Nara → Osaka

  • Days 1–2: Tokyo — Explore Shibuya, Shinjuku, Asakusa's Senso-ji Temple, Akihabara, and Tsukiji Outer Market. Take in panoramic views from the Tokyo Skytree or the observation deck at the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building.
  • Day 3: Hakone — Day trip or overnight stay in this hot spring resort town. Cruise Lake Ashi with views of Mount Fuji, ride the Hakone Ropeway, and visit the Open-Air Museum.
  • Days 4–5: Kyoto — Discover Fushimi Inari's thousand torii gates, the golden Kinkaku-ji, the bamboo groves of Arashiyama, and the geisha district of Gion. Rent a bicycle for an efficient way to explore.
  • Day 6: Nara — A short train ride from Kyoto, Nara is home to friendly free-roaming deer, the enormous Great Buddha at Todai-ji, and the serene Kasuga Grand Shrine.
  • Day 7: Osaka — End your trip in Japan's kitchen. Feast on takoyaki (octopus balls) and okonomiyaki (savory pancakes) in Dotonbori, visit Osaka Castle, and explore the vibrant Shinsekai neighborhood.

10 Days — Extended Discovery

Tokyo → Nikko → Kanazawa → Kyoto → Hiroshima → Osaka

  • Days 1–2: Tokyo — Hit the highlights of Japan's capital including Meiji Shrine, Harajuku, Akihabara, and the vibrant nightlife of Shinjuku's Golden Gai.
  • Day 3: Nikko — Day trip to the ornate Toshogu Shrine, a UNESCO World Heritage Site set amid towering cedar forests. Walk across the sacred Shinkyo Bridge and explore the Kegon Falls nearby.
  • Days 4–5: Kanazawa — Often called "Little Kyoto," Kanazawa features Kenroku-en (one of Japan's top three gardens), a beautifully preserved samurai and geisha district, and the striking 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art.
  • Days 6–7: Kyoto — Immerse yourself in temples, tea ceremonies, and traditional culture. Don't miss the Philosopher's Path, Nijo Castle, and an evening stroll through the lantern-lit streets of Higashiyama.
  • Days 8–9: Hiroshima & Miyajima — Visit the Peace Memorial Park and Museum, then take a short ferry to Miyajima Island to see the iconic floating torii gate of Itsukushima Shrine. Try Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki.
  • Day 10: Osaka — Wrap up with street food in Dotonbori, shopping in Shinsaibashi, and a visit to the excellent Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan.

14 Days — Grand Tour of Japan

Tokyo → Tohoku → Hokkaido → Kanazawa → Kyoto → Hiroshima → Kyushu

  • Days 1–2: Tokyo — Explore the capital's endless attractions, from ancient temples in Asakusa to cutting-edge technology in Odaiba. Visit Tsukiji Outer Market for the freshest sushi breakfast.
  • Days 3–4: Tohoku — Take the shinkansen north to Sendai, then explore Matsushima Bay (one of Japan's three most scenic views) and the atmospheric Yamadera mountain temple perched on a clifftop.
  • Days 5–7: Hokkaido — Fly or take the Hokkaido Shinkansen to Sapporo. Enjoy the city's famous ramen and beer scene, then venture to Otaru's charming canal district and the stunning lavender fields of Furano (summer) or world-class skiing in Niseko (winter).
  • Days 8–9: Kanazawa — Head south to explore Kenroku-en Garden, the Nagamachi samurai district, and the fresh seafood at Omicho Market. Kanazawa's craft traditions include gold leaf work and Kutani pottery.
  • Days 10–11: Kyoto — Dedicate two full days to Japan's cultural heart. Visit Kinkaku-ji, Fushimi Inari, and Arashiyama. Experience a traditional tea ceremony and wander through the atmospheric streets of Gion at dusk.
  • Day 12: Hiroshima — Pay respects at the Peace Memorial, then ferry to Miyajima Island for the floating torii gate and hiking trails on Mount Misen.
  • Days 13–14: Kyushu — Finish your journey in Japan's southern island. Explore Fukuoka's famous yatai (street food stalls) for Hakata ramen, visit the volcanic landscapes of Beppu's hot springs, or take in the dramatic scenery of Kumamoto Castle and Mount Aso.
